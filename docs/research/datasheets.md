# Datasheets — the real data Sati models learn from

One entry per dataset, written before the dataset is trained on (RP-59,
[adr/RealDataModels](../adr/RealDataModels.md)). Licence terms were read from
each source on 18 October 2026 and are quoted, not paraphrased, where they
limit use.

**The rule this file enforces:** weights trained on a dataset can be released
under Apache-2.0 only if that dataset's terms allow commercial use. Where they
allow research use only, the weights are released for research use only, or
not at all, and the model card says which. That decision is the owner's, and
it is recorded below.

## RCAEval

- **Source:** Pham et al., *RCAEval: A Benchmark for Root Cause Analysis of
  Microservice Systems* (WWW 2025). Hugging Face `phamquiluan/RCAEval`, pinned
  per run to the dataset commit. Also on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.14590730).
- **Terms:** MIT on Hugging Face, CC-BY-4.0 on Zenodo.
- **Contents:** 735 fault-injection cases on three systems: Online Boutique,
  Sock Shop and Train Ticket.
  - RE1: 375 cases, metrics only.
  - RE2: 270 cases, metrics, logs and traces.
  - RE3: 90 code-level faults.
  - Labels: root-cause service, fault type, injection time.
- **Known limits:**
  - The faults are *injected*: real systems under real load, but not organic
    outages.
  - Five candidate root-cause services per system in RE1 and RE2.
  - Some RE1-OB cases have no pre-fault window. They are kept and scored, not
    dropped.
  - The injection time is given, which is the standard RCAEval setting. A
    deployed system must detect onset itself.
- **Use here:** RE1 and RE2 are training and evaluation data, with splits that
  hold out a system, a fault type or a repetition. RE3 is never trained on.

## NAB — Numenta Anomaly Benchmark

- **Source:** github.com/numenta/NAB.
- **Terms:** MIT.
- **Contents:** 58 univariate series: real server metrics (AWS CloudWatch),
  traffic, ad clicks and tweets, plus artificial ones. Each has labelled
  anomaly windows.
- **Known limits:** Wu & Keogh (2021) show that several NAB labels are trivial,
  mislabelled or "run-to-failure". Results are reported per series family, and
  the critique is cited next to them.
- **Use here:** metric anomaly detection, with time-ordered splits within each
  series.

## SMD — Server Machine Dataset

- **Source:** Su et al., *OmniAnomaly* (KDD 2019),
  github.com/NetManAIOps/OmniAnomaly.
- **Terms:** MIT.
- **Fetched:** from the repository at commit `7fb0e0a`, pinned in `sati.detect.smd`.
- **Contents:** 28 machines × 38 metrics, five weeks, with the authors' own
  train/test split and labelled test anomalies.
- **Known limits:** labels cover anomalies the operators recognised.
  Point-adjusted F1, common in this literature, inflates scores. It is not
  used here; event-level metrics are.

## UNSW-NB15

- **Source:** Moustafa & Slay (2015), UNSW Canberra Cyber Range Lab.
- **Terms:** "Free use of the UNSW-NB15 dataset for academic research purposes
  is hereby granted in perpetuity." "Use for commercial purposes should be
  agreed by the authors." The five papers listed on the dataset page must be
  cited.
- **Contents:** flow features with labels across nine attack families. The
  official training set has 175,341 records and the testing set 82,332.
- **Known limits:** synthetic attacks generated by IXIA PerfectStorm inside a
  lab network. Some features nearly identify the attack directly, a known
  leakage risk, and they are audited before training.

## CIC-IDS2017

- **Source:** Sharafaldin, Habibi Lashkari & Ghorbani, ICISSP 2018. Canadian
  Institute for Cybersecurity, UNB.
- **Terms:** "publicly available for researchers"; cite the paper. No formal
  licence is stated.
- **Contents:** five days of captured traffic (PCAP, about 51 GB) and labelled
  CSV flows. Attacks by day:
  - Monday: benign traffic only.
  - Tuesday: FTP and SSH brute force.
  - Wednesday: DoS variants and Heartbleed.
  - Thursday: web attacks and infiltration.
  - Friday: botnet, port scan and DDoS.
- **Known limits:**
  - Published corrections (Engelen et al., 2021) found labelling and
    flow-construction errors.
  - Splitting by day means test-time attack families can be absent from
    training. That is the point of the split, and it is reported per family.

## LogHub

- **Source:** He et al., *Loghub* (ISSRE 2023), github.com/logpai/loghub. Data
  on Zenodo (record 8196385).
- **Terms:** "These log datasets are freely available for research or academic
  work."
- **Contents used:**
  - HDFS_v1: 11.2M lines over 38.7 hours, labelled anomalous blocks.
  - BGL: 4.7M lines over 214.7 days, labelled alerts.
- **Known limits:** many published HDFS results split sessions at random, which
  leaks future log templates into training. Splits here are by time.

## Kushmanda's text corpus

The language model learns only from text whose terms allow its weights to ship
under Apache-2.0. The sources are listed in `packages/sati/sati/lm/corpus.py`.

| Source | Terms | Use |
|---|---|---|
| MITRE ATT&CK (enterprise), CAPEC | MITRE's royalty-free licence to use and prepare derivative works; attribution required | Pretraining, grounded tuning, Brahmacharini |
| MITRE CWE | Free for research, development and commercial use, with MITRE's copyright notice | Pretraining, grounded tuning |
| CISA KEV | U.S. government work | Pretraining, grounded tuning, Brahmacharini |
| NVD CVE descriptions (from v2) | Descriptions under the CVE Program's terms of use: a perpetual, royalty-free licence to reproduce and prepare derivative works. NVD's own data is a U.S. government work. | Pretraining only |
| This repository's docs and course | The owner's | Pretraining |

- **NVD:** the yearly JSON 2.0 feeds from 2002 onward. Rejected CVEs and
  descriptions under 60 characters are skipped. A CVE's CWE weaknesses are
  appended to its text.
- **Known limits:** NVD descriptions are templated ("Cross-site scripting in X
  allows remote attackers to…"). MinHash near-duplicate removal collapses the
  most repetitive ones, and the cleaning statistics are printed with each run.
- **Excluded:** LogHub, which is research-use only.

## The intelligence the console serves (added 2 November 2026)

Not training data: what the API polls and the record it builds incidents from (RP-108). Terms were
read from each publisher on 2 November 2026 and are stamped on every stored record
(`Provenance.license`, `services/api/app/feeds/ingest.py`). Each incident cites the record it was
built from, so every card below can be checked one record at a time.

| Source | Terms | Collected | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| CISA KEV | CC0-1.0 | polled; whole catalogue at deploy | live (90 days) and past incidents, Sati's advisories |
| NIST NVD | public domain (U.S. government work) | polled; critical 3 years and high 1 year at deploy | live (CVSS 7.0+, 7 days) and past incidents |
| abuse.ch ThreatFox, URLhaus | abuse.ch terms; commercial use may need a Spamhaus subscription | polled with the owner's key | live botnet incidents (ThreatFox), the live feed |
| abuse.ch Feodo Tracker | CC0-1.0 | polled | live botnet incidents |
| ransomware.live | not stated by the publisher | polled; counts only shown | live ransomware incidents (no victim named) |
| ransomwatch archive | public domain (Unlicense) | at deploy | past ransomware incidents, group by month |
| Have I Been Pwned | CC BY 4.0, attribution with a visible link | polled; all breaches at deploy | breach incidents, each linking HIBP |
| MITRE ATT&CK v20.0 campaigns | ATT&CK terms of use, © The MITRE Corporation | at deploy, versioned file | past intrusion campaigns, technique by technique |
| OpenSSF malicious-packages (via OSV) | Apache-2.0 | at deploy, 8 ecosystems, 1 year | live (7 days) and past malicious packages |

**Known limits, per source.**

- **CISA KEV** — U.S.-federal in scope: it lists what CISA has evidence of, not everything
  exploited. Its ransomware flag is "Known" or "Unknown", never "No", so `unknown` is not absence.
- **NVD** — CVSS comes from more than one scorer (NVD and the reporting CNA) and they disagree; the
  record keeps the highest v3 score, as NVD's own severity filter does. Fresh CVEs sit in
  "Awaiting Analysis" with only the CNA's score. Descriptions are templated.
- **ThreatFox / URLhaus** — community reports with a reporter's confidence; one address reported
  many times is one indicator (grouped in `feeds/grouping.py`). Family names are the reporters'.
- **Feodo Tracker** — a small, curated list of botnet controllers, most of them offline at any time.
- **ransomware.live and ransomwatch** — leak-site posts are the attacker's own claims, unverified,
  sometimes duplicated or inflated; group names change and split. Counted, never named.
  ransomwatch stopped in mid-2025, so it is history only.
- **HIBP** — breaches Troy Hunt has loaded; `IsVerified` separates confirmed from reported, and
  spam lists and fabricated breaches are left out of the record.
- **ATT&CK campaigns** — 57 publicly reported intrusions, chosen by what vendors published; the
  attribution is MITRE's reading of those reports.
- **OSV malicious packages** — mostly found by automated scanners, so false positives exist; a
  withdrawn report is dropped, and only the newest 10,000 per ecosystem are kept.

## Kaal-v1 and the live-stack set (generated here)

- **Kaal-v1** — 50,000 incidents generated by `pashupatastra.kaal` (seed 1), split by fixed rules
  and committed as a manifest with hashed id lists before any training
  ([datasets/kaal-v1.json](datasets/kaal-v1.json), [adr/KaalGenerator](../adr/KaalGenerator.md)).
  Synthetic: the sim-to-real result (RP-24) is the evidence that it does not stand in for real
  incidents. The project's own; Apache-2.0.
- **Live-stack set** — 200 fault-injected incidents on an eight-service stack run in CI
  (`benchmark/live`, plan hashed before the run, RP-19). Steady load on one stack, so its
  baselines are flat; `deploy_before` gives the release fault away (RP-12 notes). The project's
  own; Apache-2.0.

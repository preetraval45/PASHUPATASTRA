## Read this first: what "our own model" means here

Being exact about this is what keeps the paper credible.

**What is realistic, and what this plan builds — the Sati model family:**

| Model | What it does | Size | Trained on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sati-Detect** | Anomaly detection on metrics | small (forest / autoencoder) | public time-series + generated telemetry |
| **Sati-RCA** | Ranks the root-cause service and fault type for an incident, citing the events behind its answer | small (gradient boosting → graph neural network) | incidents generated by Kaal + fault-injected incidents |
| **Sati-Embed** | Turns incident text into vectors for Smriti recall | small transformer encoder | generated incident pairs + runbooks |
| **Sati-LM** *(stretch)* | Writes the structured hypothesis and explanation | ~20–50M-parameter decoder, from scratch | logs, runbooks, generated narratives |

**What is not realistic:** a general-purpose LLM trained from scratch that
matches a 7B model. That takes trillions of tokens and millions of dollars of
compute. Sati-LM will be narrow — good at incident language, useless at
anything else — and that is fine, because that is its whole job.

**Sati's primary brain is the models you train from scratch. The free Oracle
Ollama box ([oracle-ollama.sh](../../scripts/oracle-ollama.sh)) has exactly two
jobs:**

1. **Backup.** If the Sati provider is down, or a Sati model has no model loaded
   for a task (Sati-LM, before Block F exists), the Gateway's existing failover
   ([failover.py](../../services/api/app/engines/providers/failover.py)) falls
   back to Ollama. Every fallback answer is labelled with the provider that
   produced it, in the response and in the audit record, so nobody mistakes an
   Ollama answer for Sati's own.
2. **Baseline to beat.** You cannot claim your model is better than an LLM
   without measuring one.

**Benchmark runs never fall back.** In an evaluation, a Sati run that fails is
recorded as a Sati failure, not quietly answered by Ollama. Otherwise the
results would credit Sati with the LLM's answers.

**Training happens on your RTX 5070 Ti.** The Oracle box is a 4-core ARM VM
with no GPU: it can serve baseline models (slowly) and run code tasks, but it
cannot train anything. Cloud GPU training is possible later, but costs money.

**The rules still hold for your own model** (CLAUDE.md):

- Sati-RCA's answer must cite events (rule 1). Unlike an LLM, a feature-based
  model can do this *by construction* — every feature comes from specific event
  IDs. That is a real advantage, and a selling point of the paper.
- Sati models are served behind the AI Gateway as a provider (rule 5), and
  return validated schemas (rule 4).
- Sati proposes, Dharma authorises (rule 2). Your own model gets no exemption.
- The engines stay deterministic. The models live in `packages/sati/` with
  their own dependencies (PyTorch). **`packages/core` stays torch-free and
  laptop-runnable.**

**Two contamination rules, which are non-negotiable:**

1. **PIB, the 20 labelled incidents, and the external benchmark are test-only.**
   Never train on them, or tune on them. A guard (RP-18) fails the build if any
   training example matches a test scenario.
2. **Splits are by topology and by fault type**, not random. A model tested on
   the same topologies it trained on has memorised the topology, and that is not
   diagnosis.

**Rules**

1. **A task is done when its *Done when* line is true.** Tick it: `- [x]`.
   Partial: `- [~]`, with what remains.
2. **Dependencies are hard.**
3. **Pre-declare before running.** Subsets, metrics, splits and answer keys are
   committed *before* results exist.
4. **Slip a week, don't skip a block.** The must-not-cut chain is: the generator
   (RP-17), then Sati-RCA (RP-24, RP-26), then the head-to-head (RP-29).
5. **The paper is yours.** Claude drafts on request; you rewrite in your own
   voice and check every number against generated tables. Disclose AI assistance
   as the venue requires.
6. **A negative result is a result.** If Sati-RCA does not beat the LLM
   baselines, the paper says so and explains why. Do not tune on the test set to
   change the answer.

**Capacity: ~12.5 h/week of your time** — Mon–Fri 1.5 h, Sat 3 h, Sun 2 h.
Training runs and long benchmark runs go overnight on your PC (🌙). Light weeks:
Thanksgiving (26 Nov) and the holidays.
